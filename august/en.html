In My Father's House and Embrace

Last month I took part in 72 hours of worship at Herrnhut, the Moravian community in Germany. As I served to prepare for the worship, I never once left the building in four days — I even forgot this was a region I had long hoped to visit, and instead remained, through worship, in the house and embrace of my Father. Looking back, I was somewhere far better than any place I could have compared it to.

Herrnhut, Church of the Brethren

Just recently I attended a seven-day united worship gathering — the Celebration for the Nations — in Wales. Watching the individuals and worship teams taking part, and reflecting on myself as well, it felt as though the Lord had called us into the middle of the wilderness to worship.

I arrived at the first day still carrying a heavy heart over problems that remained unresolved, with no answer yet for what would come after the seven days of worship. But as worship began, it felt as though the Lord was speaking clearly about what confession of faith we needed to offer in that very moment.

“Lord, I confess that You invited us into the middle of the wilderness to worship so that we might experience, most closely and clearly, Your love and faithfulness beyond all comparison. I entrust to You my whole life, before and after these seven days of worship, and I will worship — not with fear and anxiety, but with joy and gratitude — only in the house and embrace of my Father.”

Psalm 27:4
"One Thing Have I Desired" (Achat Sha'alti)

Watch the video

I only came to know the song "Achat Sha'alti" ("One Thing Have I Desired") about a month ago, but I knew at once that I had to sing it at the Celebration for the Nations — I couldn't think of a song that better captures David's situation and confession.

When David hid from King Saul in the wilderness and in caves, and again when he had to flee the palace and Jerusalem because of his son Absalom's rebellion, he grieved being far from the sanctuary — yet he longed all the more for the Lord's house, His embrace, and confessed and sang this:

Psalm 27:4 — One thing have I desired of the LORD, that will I seek after: that I may dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the LORD, and to inquire in His temple.

As we sang together, Jung Eun, the singer, had a trembling voice from the very start of her solo part. When I glanced at her face, tears were already falling without pause — I imagine David must have looked much the same.

A Life That Prepares for Revival
Simeon and Anna (Luke 2:25–38)

During the Celebration for the Nations I was interviewed about "a life that prepares for revival," and two elderly figures came to mind. Simeon, who was known as one waiting for the Messiah who would restore Jerusalem, and Anna, a widow for many years, who never left the temple — worshiping with fasting and prayer — until she saw the Messiah.

Their daily lives, ministries, and the journeys they walked were never recorded in Scripture — perhaps because no further explanation was needed. The 72 hours of worship, the worship in Wales, and every act of obedience and worship offered on the fierce battlefield of everyday life, all longing for revival, may in the end be for one single purpose:

to prepare the way in eager expectation of the Lord's return — the journey of a bride adorning herself beautifully to meet her bridegroom.

Teshuvah
תשובה / Returning, Repentance

From August 14 to September 21 is observed as the "Season of Teshuvah" — the 40-day period of repentance from the 1st of the Hebrew month of Elul to Yom Kippur, the Day of Atonement. This tradition traces back to the book of Exodus.

After the Israelites sinned by making the golden calf, Moses broke the first set of stone tablets, then climbed Mount Sinai again and fasted and interceded before God for 40 days until he received the second set of tablets (Exodus 34:4, 34:29) — and this is precisely the season now beginning.

"Whoever conceals their sins does not prosper, but the one who confesses and renounces them finds mercy" (Proverbs 28:13). I believe that meditating together on this word alongside Psalms 27 and 51 will bring deep grace of restoration.
